A warning about other calculators. Several of the top-ranking KDP spine calculators tell you to add 0.06" to the spine. Amazon's own “Create a Paperback Cover” page does not: it says spine width is page count multiplied by paper thickness, and nothing is added. The 0.06" belongs to hardcovers. Adding it to a paperback makes your cover 0.06" too wide, which is enough to have the wrap rejected or printed off-centre. This page uses Amazon's figure.

The formulas

Spine width = page count × paper thickness. Cover width = 0.125" bleed + back cover + spine + front cover + 0.125" bleed. Cover height = 0.125" + trim height + 0.125".

Inside margins change with thickness

The gutter — the inside margin, against the spine — has to grow as a book gets thicker, or the text disappears into the binding. KDP's table: 0.375" up to 150 pages, 0.5" to 300, 0.625" to 500, 0.75" to 700, and 0.875" beyond that. Outside, top and bottom need at least 0.25", or 0.375" if you use bleed.

What will it earn?

Spine width settles whether the cover prints. What it earns is a different sum: printing cost depends on page count, ink and trim size, and comes off your royalty. There is a royalty calculator here too — worth a look before you settle on a page count, because every page costs about a penny to print.

Two rules people miss

Spine text needs 79 pages. Below that KDP wants the spine left blank — the spine is too narrow to print on reliably.

Leave the barcode area clear. Keep a 2" × 1.2" clear space in the lower right of the back cover. Amazon prints a barcode there, and artwork underneath it will be covered.
