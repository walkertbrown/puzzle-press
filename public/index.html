Why your manuscript won't come back rejected

Most puzzle generators hand you a grid. KDP rejects a file for the boring reasons — wrong margins, fonts that aren't embedded, too few pages. Puzzle Press handles those before you upload.

Gutter margins by page count

KDP widens the inside margin as a book gets thicker. Puzzle Press counts your pages first, picks the right gutter, and swaps it to the correct side on left- and right-hand pages.

Six trim sizes, bleed or not

6×9, 8.5×11, 8×10, 7×10, 5.5×8.5, 5×8. Turn on bleed and the page grows by exactly what KDP asks for, with the safe area pulled in to match.

Fonts embedded and subset

An un-embedded font is one of the most common rejections. Every PDF carries its own fonts, so the file prints the same everywhere.

A predictable shape

Title page, copyright page, your puzzles, a Solutions divider, the answers packed as tightly as the page allows, and four ruled Notes pages at the back. Always the same, whatever the length — and an even page count, which KDP requires.

A cover with the right spine

Spine width is your page count times the paper thickness — get it wrong and the whole wrap is rejected. Puzzle Press knows the page count because it made the interior, so the cover comes out at the exact size, barcode area left clear.

Why the puzzles hold up

Anyone can fill a grid. These are the mistakes that earn one-star reviews — the ones a reader notices and a reviewer names — and each is designed out rather than checked for afterwards.

Word search: every word appears exactly once

Crossing words can spell a second copy of another answer by accident. Layouts that do it are thrown away and rebuilt, so a solver never finds two of the same word.

Word search: no word hidden inside another

DEER sitting inside REINDEER makes a puzzle unsolvable-feeling. They never share a grid — though both still appear across the book.

Word search: filler letters are screened

Random fill occasionally spells something you would not want in a book for children or grandparents. Every grid is checked and refilled if it does.

Mazes: exactly one route

Mazes are built as spanning trees, so there are no loops and no unreachable corners — one way in, one way out, and exactly one path between them. The answer key is correct by construction rather than by checking.

Sudoku: exactly one answer

Each puzzle is dug out one symmetric pair of clues at a time, and a clue is only removed if the grid still solves exactly one way. A sudoku with two answers would make the solutions page at the back of your own book wrong.

Every book: graded if you want

Published puzzle books usually work up from easy to hard rather than staying at one level. Pick Graded and the book does that — four bands, evenly split, with the level printed on each puzzle so a solver knows what they are in for.

Every book: no repeats

Each puzzle is generated from its own seed, so a 100-puzzle book is 100 different puzzles rather than a shuffle of the same twenty. In a word search, paste a list too short to keep that up and it tells you, instead of quietly repeating itself.

Questions people actually ask

I have never published on KDP. Where do I start?

There is a step-by-step guide on this site: choosing a puzzle type and a niche, trim size, how many puzzles, KDP's margin and font rules, the cover and spine, pricing, uploading, and the short list of mistakes that get manuscripts rejected. It is written for a first attempt and does not assume you will use this tool.

What kinds of puzzles can it make?

Word search, sudoku and mazes. Word search books draw from 32 built-in themes or a list you paste. Sudoku comes in easy, medium, hard and expert — 40, 32, 28 and 26 clues respectively, laid out with the 180-degree symmetry printed books use, and every puzzle is checked to have exactly one solution. Mazes run 15×15 up to 39×39, each one a perfect maze: every cell reachable, no loops, exactly one route from start to finish. All three also offer Graded, which is how published puzzle books are usually built: easy at the front, hardest at the back, and the level printed on each puzzle.

What margins does KDP need for my book?

The outside, top and bottom need at least 0.25 inch (0.375 inch if you use bleed). The inside — the gutter — depends on thickness: 0.375 inch up to 150 pages, 0.5 inch to 300, 0.625 inch to 500, 0.75 inch to 700, and 0.875 inch beyond that. Puzzle Press works out your final page count and applies the right one automatically. There is a free spine and cover size calculator here too, if you are checking a book you made elsewhere.

Which trim size should I pick?

6×9 inches is the most common for puzzle books and the cheapest to print. 8.5×11 gives bigger grids and suits large-print books for older solvers. Both are available here, along with 8×10, 7×10, 5.5×8.5 and 5×8.

Do I need bleed?

Only if artwork runs off the edge of the page. A plain puzzle book does not — leave bleed off. Turning it on makes the PDF page slightly larger, which KDP expects for full-bleed interiors and flags as an error otherwise.

Can I sell the books I make?

Yes. Books you generate are yours — publish them, sell them, give them away. We claim no rights to the output and take no royalty on it.

Does it make the cover too?

Yes — a full wrap with back cover, spine and front on one PDF, sized from the page count of the interior it just made. You can generate the cover of your own book for free and look at it; it comes marked PREVIEW until you pay. Spine width is page count times paper thickness, which is why the paper setting matters. KDP only allows text on the spine from 79 pages up, so shorter books get a blank one. The barcode area on the back is left clear. Cover generation is part of the paid unlock.

How many puzzles should a book have?

Most published puzzle books run 50 to 100 puzzles, whichever kind. KDP needs at least 24 pages; a 40-puzzle book with its solutions comfortably clears that. Every book ends with four ruled Notes pages, which counts towards that. Watch the royalty line as you add puzzles — every page costs about a penny to print, so a longer book is not automatically a better one.

Does anything I type get uploaded?

No. Puzzles and PDFs are built entirely in your browser. Your titles, author name and word lists are never sent to a server — the only thing that ever leaves your machine is the email you enter to unlock, which is checked against Stripe.

What if I lose the file?

Generate it again. Each book has a seed shown in the settings — put the same seed and settings back in and you get the identical book.
